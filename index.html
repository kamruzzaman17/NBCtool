Model methodology & scientific basis +

Nutrient balance framework. The model estimates the difference between total nutrient inputs and total nutrient outputs for each element (nitrogen, phosphorus, potassium, sulfur). A positive balance indicates a net surplus remaining in the soil; a negative balance indicates soil depletion requiring corrective fertilization.

Leaching losses. Leaching is estimated using element-specific multiple regression equations incorporating the applied nutrient rate, soil sand fraction, silt fraction, and clay fraction. A regression-based approach was chosen over fixed-fraction methods because leaching is strongly texture-dependent — sandy soils lose substantially more nitrogen and potassium than clay-rich soils under the same rainfall regime.

Nitrogen gaseous losses. Volatilization (NH₃) and nitrous oxide (N₂O) are modeled separately, each via its own regression equation. Volatilization coefficients include applied nitrogen rate, soil organic carbon, pH, mineral nitrogen, and seasonal rainfall. N₂O coefficients additionally incorporate soil nitrate (NO₃⁻). These two pathways are kept separate because they have distinct drivers: volatilization is dominated by pH and organic matter interactions, while N₂O production is driven by denitrification activity linked to nitrate availability.

Organic amendments. Cow dung, farmyard manure, and poultry manure each contribute fixed quantities of nitrogen, phosphorus, potassium, and sulfur per tonne, derived from average nutrient content measurements from Bangladesh field experiments. Crop residue retention contributes additional nutrients per inch of standing stubble, with crop-specific coefficients.

Zero-balance recommendation. The adjusted dose required to achieve a neutral balance is back-calculated from the balance equation, corrected by a nutrient use efficiency factor that accounts for fertilizer losses during uptake. This factor varies by crop and nutrient, reflecting differences in crop recovery efficiency across systems.

Validation. The model coefficients were derived from and validated against field experimental data collected across three agroecological zones of Bangladesh: AEZ 28 (Bogura), AEZ 30 (Cumilla), and AEZ 7 (Mymensingh), covering a range of soil textures, rainfall regimes, and agronomic management practices.

Md Kamruzzaman is a soil scientist with research interests in soil organic carbon dynamics, nitrogen cycling, nutrient biogeochemistry, and heavy metal ecotoxicology in agricultural systems. He holds an MSc in Soil Science from Bangladesh Agricultural University and is currently completing a second MSc in Ecotoxicology at the Hungarian University of Agriculture and Life Sciences (MATE), Gödöllő, Hungary, supported by the Stipendium Hungaricum Scholarship. His research spans field-scale nutrient balance modelling, greenhouse gas emissions (N₂O, CO₂) from agricultural soils, and enzyme biomarker responses to environmental contaminants.

Methodology statement: The nutrient balance equations, crop-specific regression coefficients for leaching and gaseous losses, organic amendment nutrient content constants, moisture correction factors, and agronomic efficiency parameters incorporated in this calculator were independently developed by Md Kamruzzaman, based on field experimental data collected across three agroecological zones of Bangladesh (Bogura, Cumilla, Mymensingh). This open-source web implementation is the author's own version of that work, published here to make the methodology freely accessible to researchers and practitioners.
